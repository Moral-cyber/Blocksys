\section{Discussion}

Prior-anchored vector adaptation should be interpreted through separate probability and classification endpoints. A reduction in NLL concerns assigned probabilities, whereas accuracy depends only on the highest-scoring class. Neither outcome establishes improvement in ECE or Brier score. The same-capacity zero-anchor comparison addresses whether count-derived regularization matters. Bias-only and same-prior BCTS comparisons further test how much flexibility is required. Similar behavior from the scalar-scale control would limit an explanation based on class-specific scales, rather than invalidate the broader question of local prior regularization.

Deployment state is only one part of the resource trade-off. A small affine state coexists with full-dimensional error-feedback residuals, shared-model storage, TRAIN forward passes, logit caches, and numerical fitting. Persistent-head references incur different training and storage costs. Actual codec bytes make the upload comparison reproducible, but they do not measure network delay, energy, or end-to-end deployment speed. Comparisons should retain these distinct accounting scopes.

The durable protocol addresses a separate state-consistency problem: retries must preserve the contribution whose incorporation authorizes residual commit. Its evidence comes from local recovery and replay simulations. Specification hashes do not prove decoder execution; HMAC authenticates bytes under shared keys and provides neither an embedded weight watermark nor public verification. A trusted key holder can authenticate an unhelpful or poisoned update.

The empirical scope is limited to the specified tasks, partitions, models, and fixed training seeds. Development holdouts and separately defined final-TEST client assignments answer bounded evaluation questions. They do not establish robustness across unseen heterogeneity regimes, theoretical federated convergence, or a deployed chain's performance. These boundaries preserve a testable account of predictive adaptation and upload handling without extending either result beyond its evidence.
