Abstract
Federated long-tailed learning (Fed-LT) simultaneously
tackles data heterogeneity across clients and global long-
tailed distribution challenges. Existing approaches of-
ten depend on auxiliary datasets, gradients, or statisti-
cal information, which not only increase communication
overhead but also introduce significant privacy leakage
risks. To address these issues, we first conduct a sys-
tematic empirical study of the classifier bias under imbal-
anced data. We observe that the norm imbalance prob-
lem is substantially more severe in Fed-LT than in the
centralized LT setting. Motivated by this observation, we
propose FedNPC (stochastic Noise-driven Post-hoc classi-
fier Calibration), a novel data-free post-processing calibra-
tion method for Fed-LT, using a virtual, balanced dataset.
Furthermore, we provide theoretical insights showing that
isotropic noise can effectively mitigate classifier norm im-
balance. Extensive experiments on multiple Fed-LT bench-
marks demonstrate the superior performance and gen-
eralization ability of FedNPC. Our code is available at
https://github.com/JintongGao/FedNPC.
1. Introduction
Federated Learning (FL) is a distributed machine learning
approach that has attracted widespread interest in recent
years [14, 28]. Its key idea is to allow multiple clients (such
as mobile devices or organizations) to jointly train a global
model without sharing their local datasets, thereby pro-
tecting data privacy and overcoming data isolation [7, 36].
However, traditional FL methods typically assume data dis-
tribution is idealized, which faces two major challenges
in real-world applications: (1) Global data long-tailed dis-
tribution: The overall data across all clients usually ex-
hibits a severely imbalanced (i.e., long-tailed) distribution,

where few classes have majority samples while most classes
have minority samples. (2) Local data heterogeneity: The
data distribution of each client is typically heavily Non-IID
(Non-Independent and Identically Distributed), with sig-
nificant differences in data volume and class distribution
[11, 19, 26]. The two key cruxes jointly precipitate the
emergence ofFederated Long-Tailed Learning (Fed-LT).
This critical task aims to train a generalization and unbiased
global model under the dual constraints of global long-tail
distribution and local heterogeneity [18, 34, 41].
Early Fed-LT methods simply applied traditional long-
tailed (LT) learning methods (such as re-weighting) directly
to the local training of each client [33]. However, this naive
transfer overlooks the fundamental characteristics of feder-
ated learning, which not only fails to resolve the conflicts
across client models caused by non-IID data but may exac-
erbate local biases because of its own imbalanced data dis-
tribution. Subsequently, researchers have designed methods
specifically tailored for the Fed-LT setting, focusing primar-
ily on loss function engineering [33, 39], multi-modal su-
pervision [37, 44], and classifier calibration [27, 34]. Nev-
ertheless, these dedicated methods still face various limi-
tations in practical deployment. Many methods are heav-
ily data-dependent, they require the server to access a (bal-
anced) auxiliary dataset or multi-modal supervision infor-
mation [37]. It clearly violates the core principle of fed-
erated learning (FL) that the server should be data-free.
Other calibration methods require clients to upload gradi-
ents (e.g. CReFF [35]) or feature statistics (e.g. CCVR [27])
for reconstructing information on the server side. These
approaches not only increase communication overhead but
also introduce significant privacy leakage risks. In con-
trast, a handful of existing data-free post-processing meth-
ods (such asτ-normalized [15] or Logit adjustment [29])
rely on static, global adjustments and lack the capability to
adaptively optimize model parameters for Fed-LT. We pro-

To address the aforementioned challenges, we first con-
duct an in-depth analysis of classifier bias under imbalanced
data. We perform a systematic empirical study of the classi-
fier weight norms (||wc||2 for each classc) across three sce-
narios: centralized long-tailed (LT), federated long-tailed
(Fed-LT), and the ideal balanced setting. We observe a
strong positive correlation: as the number of class sam-
plesn c decreases, the corresponding weight norm||w c||2
and test accuracya c drop sharply. More critically, we find
that local heterogeneity has a significant amplifying effect
on this bias: the imbalance problem is far more severe in
the Fed-LT setting than in the centralized LT setting (as
shown in Fig. 1a. This key finding reveals the root cause
of the poor performance. Thus, our goal becomes clear: we
aim to break this pathological coupling between data fre-
quency and classifier norms, steering the classifier from its
imbalanced state toward the ideal, balanced state of weight
norms.
In this work, we propose FedNPC (stochastic Noise-
driven Post-hoc classifier Calibration), a novel data-free
post-processing method. Our key insight is that to recali-
brate the classifier on a data-free server, we use a statisti-
cally pure and unbiased proxy for balanced data. We iden-
tify isotropic Gaussian noise (z∼ N(0, I d)) as this ideal
probe, as it avoids the inherent (and flawed) anisotropic
bias and privacy risk that real data features would intro-
duce. Thus, FedNPC operates in two stages: (1) Stage 1:
Clients perform any standard FL (or Fed-LT) training, and
the server aggregates the global model{θg,W g}. (2) Stage
2 (Post-processing): On the server, we freeze the feature ex-
tractorθ g and use this isotropic noise proxy, paired with a
uniform label distribution, to calibrate the biased classifier
W g. This approach offers key advantages: it is fully data-
free, requiring no auxiliary data, gradients, or statistics, thus
ensuring privacy and high computational efficiency. As we
analyze in Sec. 3.4, the gradient dynamics of this process
act as an adaptive norm equalizer, penalizing large norms
(head classes) and incentivizing small ones (tail classes) to
steer the classifier toward a balanced state. Finally, FedNPC
is a lightweight, plug-and-play module that can be readily
integrated into any pre-trained FL or Fed-LT model.
The contributions of this paper can be summarized: (1)
We empirically reveal that the classifier norm imbalance
problem is exacerbated in federated long-tailed learning,
leading to severe performance degradation. (2) We pro-
pose FedNPC, the first truly data-free, post-processing, and
privacy-preserving adaptive calibration method for Fed-LT.
(3) We explain why isotropic noise can effectively bal-
ance classifier norms from the view of gradient dynamics.
(4) Extensive experiments on multiple Fed-LT and stan-
dard long-tailed benchmarks demonstrate the superior per-
formance and generalization ability of FedNPC.
2. Related work
2.1. Federated learning
Federated learning has gained significant attention in re-
cent years for privacy-preserving machine learning. The
common federated classification solutions include client-
side and server-side. On the client side, many studies focus
on local optimization strategies [7, 13, 20, 22]. Server-side
optimization methods demonstrate greater potential for im-
provement. Current research primarily focuses on model
aggregation [21, 31, 36, 42] and refining the global model
[10, 23, 27] algorithms. CCVR [27] observes greater bias in
the classifier than other layers and adjusts the classifier us-
ing virtual representations sampled from an approximated
Gaussian mixture model. However, the method uploaded
the feature statistics of each client, which would violate the
privacy-preserving nature of federated learning.
2.2. Long-tailed learning
Long-tailed classification learning presents a significant
challenge in real-world scenarios. Effective solutions to
the imbalance problem can be divided into re-sampling
[6, 16, 45], re-weighting [2, 8, 32], and re-training meth-
ods [1, 15, 24, 29]. Among them, recent studies relevant to
our work have explored classifier re-training methods. For
instance, Kanget al. [15] demonstrated thatl 2 norms of
the weight vectors are biased by the training data distribu-
tion on long-tailed (LT) classification. They proposed de-
coupling the training and re-training the classifier (e.g. cRT,
τ-normalized). However, these methods are designed for
centralized settings and usually require access to data for
re-calibration. In contrast, our work first identifies that this
norm bias leads to exacerbated performance degradation in
Fed-LT, and then proposes a data-free, post-hoc calibration
method suitable for the privacy constraints of FL.
2.3. Federated long-tailed learning
Federated long-tailed learning has emerged as a critical
problem with data heterogeneity and global data long-tailed
distribution. Recent researches are broadly categorized into
several directions: loss function engineering [33, 39, 40],
multi-modal supervision [37, 44], classifier calibration, and
others [34, 41]. Loss function engineering methods address
data imbalance by adjusting class weights or incorporating
regularization. Multi-modal supervision methods leverage
cross-modal knowledge to enhance representations or logit
vectors. Classifier re-training methods decouple feature
learning and classifier optimization for balanced classifier
weights [35, 37]. CReFF [35] re-trains a global classifier
using learnable federated features through gradient match-
ing on the server.

3.2. Problem: Imbalanced norms and exacerbated
performance bias in Fed-LT
To diagnose the core problem in Fed-LT, we conduct an em-
pirical study comparing model accuracy (a c) and classifier
weight norms (||w c||2) for each classcon CIFAR-100 un-
der three settings: Fed-LT (via FedAvg under different non-
IIDnessα), centralized LT, and centralized Balanced. As
shown in Fig. 1, the balanced setting yields uniform norms
and accuracy. In contrast, both LT settings show a down-
ward slope: head classes have larger norms and higher ac-
curacy, while tail classes have smaller norms and lower ac-
curacy. The Pearson correlation between||w c||2 anda c is
strong in LT (0.989) and Fed-LT (0.967 average under dif-
ferentα), but near zero in the Balanced setting (0.069).
Crucially, the performance bias is significantly aggra-
vated in Fed-LT, exhibiting a steeper accuracy decline com-
pared to centralized LT (Fig. 1a). See Appendix Figs. 10
and 11 for more results across different IF andα. This con-
firms that local heterogeneity exacerbates the bias stemming
from the global imbalance. Based on this evidence, we for-
malize the problem:
Assumption 1(Imbalanced Norm Postulate).In a model
trained under Fed-LT, class sample sizen c, final classi-
fierl 2-norm||w c||2, and class-wise test accuracya c are
strongly positively correlated:n c ↑=⇒ ||w c||2 ↑=⇒
ac ↑.
Supported by Fig. 1, Assumption 1 implies data imbal-
ancen c induces biased norms||w c||2, causing imbalanced
performancea c. Our goal is to break this coupling. We con-
trast this with the ideal state (equal norms) observed empir-
ically (Fig. 1b, Balanced) and established theoretically by
Neural Collapse (NC) [30]:

Theorem 1 clarifies our objective: decouple||wc||2 fromn c

3.3. The proposed method: FedNPC
Section 3.2 identified the problem: imbalanced norms
linked to exacerbated bias in Fed-LT, contrasting with the
ideal goal of balanced norms (Theorem 1). How can we
steer the biased classifierW g toward this goal under FL
constraints? Using balanced auxiliary data or feature statis-
tics (N(µ,Σ)) may face critical issues: (1) Privacy & Prac-
ticality: Violates the server’s data-free principle and client
privacy (e.g. CCVR [27], CReFF [35]). (2) Potential Bias:
Real data’s inherent anisotropy (Σ̸=σ 2I) can interfere
with norm equalization, as suggested by experiments in
Fig. 4.

Can a statistically pure, perfectly balanced, isotropic proxy

3.5. Privacy discussion
FedNPC is inherently more privacy-preserving than prior
calibration methods like CCVR [27] and CReFF [35]. It op-
erates entirely server-side using only the aggregated model
from standard FL, requiring no auxiliary datasets or sensi-
tive client information (gradients or statistics). Stage 2 in-
volves no client communication. This eliminates additional
privacy risks and communication overhead, aligning with
FL principles while maintaining efficiency (Table 6). Its
plug-and-play nature makes it suitable for real-world Fed-
LT scenarios.
4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental setting
Datasets.We conduct experiments on the following bench-
mark datasets for federated long-tailed learning, including
CIFAR-10-LT [4], CIFAR-100-LT [4], and ImageNet-LT
[5]. CIFAR-10/100-LT datasets are derived from CIFAR-
10/100 datasets [17] employing an exponential distribu-
tion governed by the Imbalance Factor (IF) between the
most and least frequent classes. With an imbalance factor
IF=256, ImageNet-LT is a modified version of ImageNet
[5] containing 1,000 unevenly distributed classes. In fed-
erated long-tailed tasks, all experiments are split for non-
IID datasets implemented using the Dirichlet distribution
approach, where parameterαcontrols the level of non-
IIDness. We setαas 0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 along with imbalance
factors (IF) of 100, 50, and 10 on CIFAR-LT. We setα=0.1
with IF=256 on ImageNet-LT.
Implementation details.All experiments are imple-
mented using PyTorch with an SGD optimizer (momen-
tum=0.9) on the Tesla A10 GPU. For a fair comparison,
we follow prior work [35] by using ResNet-8 [9] backbone
for CIFAR-10/100-LT and ResNet-50 [9] for ImageNet-LT.
We report test accuracy following standard evaluation pro-
tocols. In federated learning, we use the standard cross-
entropy loss for 200 communication rounds with 20 total
clients (K=20), randomly selecting 40% of clients per round
with a learning rate of 0.1. Each client performs 10 local
training epochs. In stage 2, we set the learning rate to 0.1,
with the training sample sizeMof 10,000. The models are
trained for 28 epochs on CIFAR-10-LT and 50 epochs on
CIFAR-100-LT.
Baselines.In our study, we compare against the fol-
lowing baseline approaches: (1) Federated learning meth-
ods: FedAvg [28], FedProx [20], FedAvgM [10], FedDF
[23], FedBE [3], FedNova [38], FedDisco [42], CCVR
[27], and FedLWS [36]; (2) Federated long-tailed meth-
ods: Fed-Focal Loss [33], Ratio Loss [39], FEDIC [34],
CReFF [35], Fed-GraB [40], Fed-LoGe [41], and FedLF
[25]; (3) Long-tailed methods: Empirical risk minimization
(ERM, training on the cross-entropy loss),τ-normalized
[15], cRT [15], post-hoc logit adjustment [29], MaxNorm
[1], BALMS [32], and LOS [24].

where variances vary across dimensions) undermines the
norm equalizer. The uniform distribution with its funda-

with our framework. Meanwhile, Rademacher noise as dis-

to effectively regularize and calibrate the model. It confirms
that Isotropic Gaussian noise is the most effective choice for
achieving an ideal, balanced state. It not only validates the
theoretical framework of our method but also demonstrates
its robustness.

5. Conclusion
By comprehensive theoretical analysis and extensive ex-
periments, we demonstrate that the classifier norm im-
balance problem is exacerbated in Fed-LT. We present a
novel stochastic noise-driven post-hoc classifier calibration
method for equalizing norms. Unlike existing approaches
that require auxiliary datasets or feature statistics, Fed-
NPC is a data-free, post-processing, and privacy-preserving
adaptive calibration method. Extensive experiments with
various Fed-LT benchmarks demonstrate the remarkable
performance and generalization ability of FedNPC.
