# Residual log-prior anchor: mechanism review

Native assessment by `/root/route_a_stats`, 2026-11-05. Planning only: no candidate code, fitting, or new score has run. This is one bounded ScientistTwo hypothesis check, using the local native-agent adaptation described in `06_ScientistTwo/README.md`, not an upstream autonomous campaign or external review.

**Question.** Does the frozen shared predictor already encode class-frequency information, so shrinking its local bias toward `center(log pi)` counts that information twice? Candidate: replace the bias center by `mu = center(log pi - log q)`, keeping the same 20-dimensional vector predictor, ridge 10 and TRAIN-only fitting.

**Mathematical verdict: a familiar ratio, with an unidentified denominator.** Under label shift, with unchanged class-conditionals and a calibrated source posterior, Bayes gives `p_target(c|x) ∝ p_source(c|x) pi_c/rho_c`. Thus the fixed logit correction `log pi - log rho` is standard prior-ratio adaptation. It is not a new mechanism. At infinite regularization, the proposed ratio-centered vector becomes precisely this fixed offset when `q=rho`. At finite regularization it is ordinary ridge calibration around that offset: writing `b=mu+r` gives scores `A*z+mu+r` with penalty on `r`, retaining the same affine predictor capacity and familiar shrinkage principle. PRIOR/pFedBreD already establishes personalized regularization centers at the model-training level; changing only this center cannot support a generic novelty claim.

Two denominator definitions must remain separate. A **local** predictive marginal `q_i = mean_{x in local TRAIN} softmax(z(x))` uses the target client's input mixture. It is generally `C_soft*pi_i`, not the source prior `rho`. In a calibrated binary example with source prior `(0.5,0.5)` and class-conditional feature probabilities `(0.8,0.2)`, target prior `(0.9,0.1)` gives local predicted mass `q_i=(0.644,0.356)`: the required odds multiplier is 9, whereas `pi_i/q_i` supplies about 4.98. This is a mathematical illustration, not an experiment. A **global pooled TRAIN** marginal can approximate `rho` only if the model is calibrated on that pool and the pool represents its source distribution; the current FedLC-inspired loss does not establish either condition. Sharing pooled statistics also adds a final collection/distribution step, so it cannot silently inherit the original zero-extra-uplink statement.

**Closest sources actually inspected.** Saerens, Latinne and Decaestecker (2002), DOI `10.1162/089976602753284446`: the retrieved publisher/Crossref abstract describes output correction for changed class priors and EM estimation without refitting; the formula above is our Bayes derivation, not a claim to have read an unavailable equation. Menon et al., *Long-Tail Learning via Logit Adjustment*, inspected v2 full text §3/Eq.8 and §4: subtracting source log class priors from logits is a classic post-hoc correction; their balanced-error objective differs from our local NLL. Shi et al., *PRIOR*, inspected v2 Eq.8/11/14 and Algorithm1: personalized means center Bregman/model regularization; they do not supply evidence that a local average predicted probability is a source prior. Exact local excerpts and hashes are in `LITERATURE_EXTRACT.json`.

**Decision recommendation.** Low novelty as a standalone contribution. The proposed local `q_i` is defensible only as a heuristic **residual-anchor hypothesis**, not an identified label-shift correction or proof that double counting occurs. A small development screen could reject that heuristic or explain when the original center already works. It should not trigger full promotion, new official TEST evaluation, or replacement of protected results on its own. The neighboring covariance-geometry and support-aware candidates from the limitation/idea agent may address a more distinct mechanism; those need their own fixed plans.
