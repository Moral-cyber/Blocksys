\begin{abstract}
Compressed updates cut uplink traffic, but a shared model averaged over label-skewed clients can rank classes poorly for individual clients. We fit a twenty-param\-eter post-hoc predictor per client on frozen logits and shrink its bias toward the client's own class prior rather than toward zero. At the optimum $\lambda(b-\mu)=n-\sum p$: the bias stays centered when fitted probability mass matches observed counts and moves otherwise. Over three fixed seeds, client-macro negative log-likelihood (NLL) falls from 0.680 to 0.645 (CIFAR-10) and 0.206 to 0.188 (FashionMNIST), with the gain in every seed. Permuting the prior's class labels preserves norm and capacity but increases NLL in all 60 client-seed pairs per task relative to the aligned center; its mean also exceeds the zero-center mean. Three-bit uploads cost 54,067 B, 90.1\% below dense; the predictor adds 80 B of local state.
\keywords{Federated learning; label skew; post-hoc calibration; prior regularization}
\end{abstract}
