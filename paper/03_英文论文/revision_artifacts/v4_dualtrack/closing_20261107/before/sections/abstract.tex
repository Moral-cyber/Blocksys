\begin{abstract}
Federated clients differ in their label distributions, and the updates that carry their models are compressed before they leave the device. We treat these as two halves of one system. For prediction, each client fits a twenty-parameter affine predictor on frozen shared logits and shrinks its bias toward its own class prior rather than toward zero, so the fit keeps the prior where fitted probability mass agrees with local counts and overrides it where it does not. Client-macro NLL falls from 0.680 to 0.645 on CIFAR-10 and from 0.206 to 0.188 on FashionMNIST over three fixed seeds, and in a three-point sweep the mean gain rises from 0.004 to 0.041 on CIFAR-10 as heterogeneity strengthens. For transmission, each three-bit update is bound to a ledger record; an authenticated incorporation receipt ties the encoded payload and its round context to the resulting model state, and a receipt-gated protocol commits the error-feedback residual only after incorporation. Fifteen registrations across five clients and three rounds cost 4.23 M gas, and every resulting model matches the direct path bit for bit.
\keywords{Federated learning; label heterogeneity; compact personalization; ledger anchoring}
\end{abstract}
